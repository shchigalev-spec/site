# A-Modul Direct V2 — generated image bible

Internal visual direction: **Controlled Launch**. This phrase is not a public product name.

## Narrative anchor

One remote site progresses through a credible, finite industrial sequence:

`empty terrain → planning grid → BIM composition → production → logistics → installation → operational object`

The visual system should make project control legible. It is not a gallery of unrelated buildings and must never imply that a generated image documents a real customer project.

## Brand basis

The official Avista SVG logo uses deep purple `#492D7D` and magenta `#E40A46`. The application may extend these with deep graphite, warm industrial off-white, restrained steel grey, and one quiet technical secondary colour. No blue-purple SaaS glow and no generic construction-yellow safety treatment.

Typography:

- Geologica for display;
- Onest for body and controls;
- IBM Plex Mono for regions, project data, dimensions, and statuses;
- never Inter.

## Image language

- High-end industrial architectural CGI with believable modular-construction logic.
- Russian remote-region landscapes: taiga edges, open industrial sites, subdued northern terrain, winter-road or coastal logistics only where context requires it.
- Clean factory environments, rational structural assemblies, and plausible transport/installation choreography.
- Precise geometry, straight verticals, consistent module proportions, restrained material detail.
- Controlled daylight or practical industrial lighting; premium B2B editorial restraint.
- Strong negative space for live HTML copy; no baked text, labels, numbers, logos, measurements, maps, UI, or buttons.
- Consistent lens family and camera height within every sequential set.
- Every case visualization carrying real project facts must show the live caption: `Визуализация состава объекта. Факты приведены по реализованному проекту.`

## Global style-anchor protocol

1. Generate three global anchor candidates using the same core brief but distinct framing.
2. Inspect each at full resolution for module joints, structural rhythm, vehicles, cranage, roads, and repeated/deformed elements.
3. Select one anchor; record the selection and rejection reasons.
4. Generate route anchors from the selected palette, lens, weather, materials, and geometry.
5. Reject any route image that reads as a different campaign, a luxury resort, documentary photography, or generic stock render.
6. Build aligned sequential plates for the hero with locked terrain, road, camera, light, building orientation, and module geometry.
7. Create dedicated desktop and mobile crops; do not rely on one crop for both.
8. Keep generation masters outside production, then export correctly sized AVIF/WebP derivatives with intrinsic dimensions.
9. Record every English production prompt and output mapping in `A-MODUL-IMAGE-PROMPTS.md` and `A-MODUL-GENERATED-ASSET-MANIFEST.md`.

## Camera and composition

- Route hero desktop: wide 16:9 or slightly wider architectural composition with the primary site mass outside the copy-safe region.
- Route hero mobile: purpose-generated or carefully art-directed portrait crop where the object remains identifiable behind normal-flow text.
- Project sequences: fixed three-quarter elevated camera, consistent horizon and road position, no drone-tour spectacle.
- Factory sequence: consistent bay, camera, frame geometry, and light direction as the module progresses through finite stages.
- Interiors: architectural visualization, human-scale but unoccupied or minimally populated; no identifiable faces, fake signage, or lifestyle-ad staging.

## Material and environment anchors

- Graphite and muted steel frames.
- Warm off-white or restrained cool-grey envelope panels.
- Official purple/magenta used as controlled wayfinding or component accents, never painted across every building.
- Plausible gravel pads, utility corridors, foundations, service routes, weathering, and connection points.
- Landscapes support logistics and climate context without turning into tourism imagery.

## Continuity checklist

Sequential images must preserve all of the following:

- camera position, focal length, and horizon;
- terrain profile and surface;
- road/track position;
- building orientation and footprint;
- module proportions, façade bay rhythm, window/door placement;
- light direction and weather family;
- palette and material family;
- crane/truck presence only when the depicted stage needs it.

The hero sequence requires four aligned states: clean empty site, planning-grid state, partial settlement, completed operational settlement.

## Route families

### General modular buildings

A serious remote industrial complex assembled from several building functions. The visual must suggest flexible object scope—not a single retail container. General hero, BIM, factory, logistics, dominant case, final CTA, and social card must feel like one campaign.

### Shift camps

A coherent settlement plan with dormitories, canteen, ABK/BPK, medical/security/support buildings, transitions, and service infrastructure. The Kamchatka case visualization must use verified composition facts without pretending to be the actual project photograph.

### Offices and ABK

Factory-ready administrative buildings with rational entries, circulation, meeting/work areas, sanitary and technical support. Avoid glass-cube corporate architecture and luxury-office styling.

### Dormitories

Personnel housing with credible room, sanitary, household, furniture, and engineering context. The 300-person case family must be architectural visualization tied to the locked numeric facts only.

### Factory and logistics

Finite factory stages: metal, frame, envelope, engineering, finishing, quality control, shipment. Logistics modes: road, rail, sea, and winter road; only depict a mode when a selected region or real case makes it relevant.

## Hard reject list

- fantasy megastructures or luxury resorts;
- impossible joints, floating volumes, arbitrary cantilevers, duplicated modules;
- deformed trucks/cranes, meaningless heavy machinery, random worker poses;
- unreadable signs, embedded copy, generated logos, customer logos;
- fake maps, charts, routes, dimensions, or interface panels;
- cyberpunk, blue/purple neon, particles, smoke, exaggerated sparks;
- military compounds or prison-like visual language;
- yellow/black stripe cliché;
- cheap stock-render staging or unrelated camera/weather sets;
- any image that could reasonably be mistaken for documentary case photography without the required visualization label.

## Raster delivery rules

- No video, animated raster, GIF, or autoplay media.
- Production receives AVIF/WebP and responsive crops only; masters remain out of shipped assets.
- Below-fold media loads lazily and uses intrinsic dimensions.
- Do not download or hotlink random external imagery.
- The official Avista logo is the only non-generated primary brand asset; it must be obtained from the official site and not redrawn.

## Milestone A anchor selection — 2026-08-25

Three 16:9 global anchors were generated and inspected at full resolution. Candidate C is the selected continuity anchor.

- Candidate A was rejected: a small unreadable pseudo-sign appeared on the nearest volume, and the dense industrial superstructure weakened the modular-object reading.
- Candidate B was rejected: geometry was clean, but the dispersed campus read too close to a catalogue of isolated accommodation blocks.
- Candidate C was selected: its locked diagonal service road, quiet left field, rational repeated bays, restrained purple/magenta components, and three-building composition support a legible `empty → plan → assembly → operation` sequence.

The selected scene was edited into an empty prepared site and a partial-settlement state. The planning state uses the empty-site raster with an HTML/SVG project grid, so no labels or interface graphics are baked into imagery. Desktop and mobile AVIF/WebP derivatives are generated independently; the full-resolution masters remain outside the production application.
